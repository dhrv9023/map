Hard Rules

  • Do phases in order. Phase 1 weak → everything after will be garbage.
  • DSA every single day. No exceptions. 1 LeetCode problem minimum in C++.
  • Build every project listed. Watching tutorials ≠ learning.
  • Use C++ for DSA, Python for ML/AI work.
  • Target: 3–5 hours/day. Realistic, not wishful.
  • Phase 1 completion test: can you deploy a Dockerized FastAPI + PostgreSQL service?
  • Phase 4 completion test: can you write a CUDA kernel and explain its roofline chart?

The Beginner's 4-Step "Stuck Protocol"

  • Step 1: Pen & Paper First (5 min): Never touch the keyboard immediately. Write down a tiny sample array (e.g. [2, 7, 11, 15], target 9). Walk through how your human eyes solve it.
  • Step 2: Shameless Brute Force (10 min): Write the naive solution first! A working nested loop with O(N²) is infinitely better than staring at an empty editor. Getting test cases to pass builds immediate confidence.
  • Step 3: Spot the Bottleneck: Ask: "Which part of my brute force is slow?" Example: "I am searching for (target - x) across the array in O(N). Can I do it in O(1)?" → Hash map discovered!
  • Step 4: Refactor to C++ Optimal (15 min): Replace the slow search with the optimal data structure, check edge cases (empty array, single element, negative numbers), and submit.

How to Answer a System Design Question

  • Step 1 (5 min): Clarify requirements. Functional + non-functional. Ask QPS, storage, latency, availability targets.
  • Step 2 (5 min): High-level design. Draw the boxes. Don't overdesign yet.
  • Step 3 (15 min): Deep dive into 2–3 components. This is where you show depth.
  • Step 4 (5 min): Bottlenecks and tradeoffs. What breaks at 10x scale? How do you fix it?
  • For AI Infra roles: always bring it back to GPU utilization, latency, throughput, and cost.
